\begin{abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
% ===================== ABSTRACT (inlined) ============================
This dissertation presents original research on the dynamics of a
financial knowledge graph built from public news: how fast relationships between
companies, sectors, countries and themes form (velocity), how that rate
changes (acceleration), and whether that signal predicts what forms next.
A knowledge graph represents entities and the relationships between them; a
time-indexed knowledge graph additionally records when each relationship forms,
making its dynamics measurable.
The hypothesis is that the rate at which entities become connected leads which
connections form, treated as a graph-native question under strict point-in-time
integrity. 

The work comprises three experiments:

\textbf{Experiment 1: Building the Time-Indexed Knowledge Graph.} The
substrate is constructed by running an open-source LLM extractor over the
FNSPID news corpus at two time-stratified scales, producing a
FinDKG-compatible temporal knowledge graph; a syndication-duplication
audit motivates the deduplicated core on which every headline analysis
runs.

\textbf{Experiment 2: Velocity, Bursts, and Walk-Forward Theme Discovery.}
A walk-forward system is created that
scores each entity's link-formation acceleration causally, clusters
co-accelerating entities into themes, and tracks them into dated
lifelines. Against a frozen ten-event gold list it detects 8/10 major
2020--2023 themes at a median lead of $-1$~week (placebo expectation
$\sim$2/10, $p \approx 10^{-4}$), and its discovery stream is audited
for precision, template-filtered, and distilled into a lightweight
judge.

\textbf{Experiment 3: Temporal Link Prediction under Point-in-Time
Discipline.} The third experiment maps what is and is not forecastable
at the individual-edge level, benchmarking a temporal graph network
against static, heuristic, recurrence and semantic baselines under a
raw-ranking protocol. Edge-level forecasting proves bimodal: recurring
relationships are highly predictable while novel ones sit near a low
ceiling, and the detector's own velocity signal degrades forecasting
when injected into the network, so velocity is a detection signal
rather than a forecasting feature.

The dissertation makes four contributions:

\textbf{1. Leakage-controlled temporal knowledge graph} in
FinDKG-compatible format, with themes as CONCEPT and EVENT nodes and a
reproducible point-in-time audit trail, where graph and feature leakage are
prevented by design and extractor hindsight is measured rather than assumed away.

\textbf{2. Validated walk-forward theme-discovery system}: a definition and
measurement of link-formation velocity and acceleration on a temporal knowledge
graph, a three-detector burst account of emergence, and a causal
theme-discovery system with data-driven noise controls, validated by lead time
against a frozen gold list and by a placebo control, audited for
lifeline-level precision by a validated language-model judge, refined by
an audit-driven template-article filter at no cost to recall, and
distilled into a deployable lightweight judge.

\textbf{3. Decisive, replicated benchmark of edge-level predictability}:
establishing that, under point-in-time discipline, recurrence dominates,
a temporal graph network improves on static structure only as data density
grows, and a recurrence$\rightarrow$ChronoBERT backoff is the strongest system,
with the method ranking replicated across three graph constructions,
extractor lookahead bias bounded below measurement resolution, and a
velocity-feature ablation showing that the detection signal does not transfer
to edge-level forecasting.

\textbf{4. GitHub repository provides source code and methodology},
together with the derived knowledge-graph dataset (triples and indices
only; no article text is redistributed), released to enable replication.
% =====================================================================
\end{abstract}

\chapter*{Impact Statement}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Impact Statement}
\textbf{Experiment 1: Building the Time-Indexed Knowledge Graph.} By
building the substrate from open
data with an open-source extractor, and by releasing the code and the
derived knowledge-graph dataset (no source article text is
redistributed), the work lowers the barrier for practitioners and
regulators to reproduce and extend these capabilities, and demonstrates
that open tooling under disciplined point-in-time evaluation can rival
closed, vendor-supplied alternatives.

\textbf{Experiment 2: Velocity, Bursts, and Walk-Forward Theme Discovery.}
The walk-forward detector
produces a systematic, auditable signal for detecting emerging
investment themes before they are widely reported: because every edge is
timestamped by publication date and evaluated under strict point-in-time
discipline, the signal is suitable for production use by quantitative
asset managers, complementing existing factor models rather than
replacing them. Velocity and burst detection on a temporal financial
knowledge graph extend naturally to risk and compliance applications
such as early detection of contagion in counterparty networks,
ESG-controversy monitoring, and sector-rotation or macro-regime timing.

\textbf{Experiment 3: Temporal Link Prediction under Point-in-Time
Discipline.} The edge-level benchmark tells
practitioners what such graphs can and cannot deliver: recurring
relationships are predictable, genuinely novel ones are close to
unforecastable, and burst signals belong in detection rather than in
forecasting models. This calibration of expectations, together with the
point-in-time evaluation discipline it rests on, transfers to any
financial machine-learning pipeline built on news-derived data.

Any direct investment application is treated throughout as a downstream
use of the method rather than its aim.

\section{Research Motivations}
Investment themes reshape markets, but they are usually recognised only once
they have been widely reported. By the time a theme such as generative AI is named in commentary and research notes, the
attention-based indicators that analysts watch (headline counts, search
interest, sentiment scores) have already reacted, and much of the
informational advantage has dissipated. The relationships among companies,
sectors, countries and themes, however, begin to change before a theme is
named: firms are linked to a new concept, concepts are linked to one another,
and the density of those connections grows. If that structural change can be
observed as it happens, the rate at which relationships form may
anticipate what attention-based indicators only confirm after an event occurs.

Existing financial knowledge graphs are not well suited to answering this
question. They are largely static or transductive, scoring whether a relation
plausibly exists rather than when and how quickly relations form, and they
rarely audit point-in-time correctness, which is the central threat to validity
in any financial study. This dissertation therefore investigates whether
the velocity of relationship formation, measured on a graph built under
strict point-in-time discipline, carries information about which relationships
form next.

We measure and characterise the velocity signal by defining it, smoothing it
without looking ahead, and detecting when it bursts; we then use that signal to
discover emerging themes as they form, and separately benchmark how far
individual future links can be predicted at all, against strong baselines and
evaluate the results.

\section{Research Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}
The main objective of this research is to determine whether the dynamics of a
financial knowledge graph anticipate the formation of new relationships, under
conditions strict enough that a positive result could be trusted. To this end a
time-indexed knowledge graph is built from public financial news; the velocity
and acceleration of edge formation are defined and measured; emerging themes are
detected as bursts in that signal and validated against known episodes; and the
forecastability of individual future links is benchmarked against strong
baselines.

Point-in-time integrity is the central methodological commitment, and the work
distinguishes two kinds of leakage. Graph and feature leakage are prevented by
design: every edge is dated by the article's publication date rather than by the
date of the event it describes, which is the single most important control.
Alongside it stand chronological train/validation/test splits, structural masking (at time
$t$ the model observes only edges with timestamp $\leq t$), rolling causal
normalisation, a conservative raw-ranking evaluation, within-week
update-after-scoring ordering, and validation-only hyperparameter and threshold
tuning. Extractor hindsight is measured rather than eliminated: the
language-model extractor used to build the graph carries world-knowledge of the
study period, so the residual is quantified through a blinded-versus-unblinded
diagnostic and a cutoff-controlled comparison against chronologically
consistent models, and reported as bounded-and-measured rather than assumed
absent.

We use the ICKG relation extractor; success is measured on graph-native metrics
rather than as an investment signal; and country (GPE) relations, though
present, are sparse owing to the US-centricity of the corpus and are documented
as a limitation.

\section{Research Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
The dissertation builds its substrate first, then characterises the velocity
signal, and finally tests its predictive value. It is split into three
experiments:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Building the Time-Indexed Knowledge Graph.} The first experiment
constructs the substrate rather than consuming a pre-built graph. The
open-source ICKG extractor (ICKG-v4.2, a LoRA adapter on Qwen-2.5-14B-Instruct
distilled from GPT-4) is run over the FNSPID full-text news corpus to produce
FinDKG-compatible quadruples (subject, relation, object, time) over twelve entity
and fifteen relation types, with themes realised as CONCEPT and EVENT nodes and
every edge timestamped by publication date. Extraction is clean at scale
(0\% malformed output; $\sim$18.5 valid triples per article). The graph
is built at two corpus
scales (201{,}600 and 604{,}800 time-stratified articles), and a data-hygiene
audit shows that 10--24\% of corpus articles are verbatim syndication
duplicates whose removal halves the denoised core; the deduplicated core
(21{,}216 entities, 212{,}294 edge-weeks) is adopted as the canonical
graph and the smaller corpus retained as a replication
sample.

\item \textbf{Edge-Formation Velocity, Burst Detection, and Walk-Forward Theme
Discovery.} The second experiment formally defines and
measures the rate and acceleration of new-edge formation per node, relation, and
theme using trailing-window, past-only Poisson-surprise normalisation; it
detects bursts with three independent detectors (Kleinberg's automaton, the
CUSUM control chart, and Bayesian online changepoint detection) so that
detected episodes are not an artefact of one algorithm; and it clusters co-accelerating entities on their recent
co-occurrence subgraph into themes, tracked week-over-week into lifelines whose
birth week is the date the system would have flagged the theme live. Two
data-driven noise controls (template-article fingerprinting and a habituation
score separating emerging themes from recurring rituals) require no hand
curation. Detections are validated against a frozen ten-event gold list with
event-anchored birth windows, and specificity is established with a
100{,}000-draw placebo control: the system detects 8/10 major 2020--2023
themes at a median lead of $-1$~week against a placebo expectation of
$\sim$2/10 ($p \approx 10^{-4}$), with anticipatable themes coming early
(the COVID market crisis $-3$~weeks, the vaccine race $-8$) while pure
shocks sit at the news-flow floor ($+1$). A lifeline-level precision audit, judged
blind against source articles by a validated locally hosted language model,
measures the share of genuine storylines in the full discovery stream across
four configurations, putting the high-recall stream's precision at
37.9\% and raising it to 44.7\% under an audit-driven template filter
with the gold-list result unchanged. The
audit's labels are then reused twice, as a fine-tuned lightweight judge
that passes the same validation gate as its teacher, and as stream
filters whose recall cost the gold-list machinery prices directly.

\item \textbf{Temporal Link Prediction under Point-in-Time Discipline.} The
third experiment maps what is and is not forecastable at the individual-edge
level, contextualising the theme system. An RE-GCN-style temporal graph network
is evaluated against the ComplEx static embedding, Common-Neighbours
and Adamic--Adar heuristics, a training-free recurrence baseline and a
ChronoBERT semantic baseline, using MRR and Hits@$k$ under a conservative
raw-ranking protocol with a recurring/novel decomposition, bootstrap confidence
intervals and seed replication; the same benchmark is run on all three graph
constructions to test replication. Recurring relationships prove highly
predictable while genuinely novel ones sit near a low ceiling for every
method; the strongest system is a recurrence$\rightarrow$ChronoBERT
backoff; the temporal network's advantage over static structure appears
only as data density grows, a qualified null reported as a substantive
finding; and a cutoff-controlled comparison bounds extractor lookahead
bias below measurement resolution ($<0.002$ MRR). A velocity-feature
ablation then injects
the detector's burst statistics into the temporal network, testing whether
the discovery signal doubles as a forecasting feature; a complementary
retraining on the template-filtered graph tests whether extraction-layer
cleaning propagates to forecasting. The ablation significantly degrades
forecasting on every subset, and the retraining shows per-edge skill
barely moving while the aggregate benchmark falls: velocity is a
detection signal rather than a forecasting feature, and template noise
inflates apparent forecastability.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions to Science}
\label{sec:contributions}
The thesis presents the following contributions to science:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A built, leakage-controlled temporal knowledge graph, with a
corpus-hygiene audit.} A time-indexed financial knowledge graph constructed
from public news in FinDKG-compatible quadruple format at two corpus scales,
with themes as CONCEPT and EVENT nodes and a reproducible point-in-time audit
trail; graph and feature leakage are prevented by design, extractor hindsight
is measured rather than assumed away, and a syndication-duplication audit
(10--24\% verbatim duplicate articles in FNSPID) quantifies and corrects a
bias affecting any per-ticker news corpus.

\item \textbf{A validated walk-forward theme-discovery system.} A definition
and measurement of link-formation velocity and acceleration on a temporal
knowledge graph; a three-detector burst account of emergence; and a causal
theme-discovery system (acceleration $\rightarrow$ community clustering
$\rightarrow$ tracked lifelines, with data-driven noise controls) validated by
lead time against a frozen gold list of ten documented episodes and by a
placebo control. The finding is that lead time reflects the information
structure of the event: anticipatable themes are detected weeks early and
shock events sit at the news-flow floor. The discovery stream is further
audited at the lifeline level by a blind, control-validated language-model

recall cost is measured on the same frozen gold list.

\item \textbf{A decisive, replicated benchmark of edge-level predictability.}
Establishing that, under point-in-time discipline, recurrence dominates what is
predictable, genuinely novel edges sit near a low ceiling for every method, a

cutoff-controlled comparison. A velocity-feature ablation shows that the

shared test queries, that template noise inflates apparent

\item \textbf{A GitHub repository provides source code and methodology.}
The repository releases the source code and the derived knowledge-graph
dataset (no source article text), enabling replication and extension.
\end{enumerate}

In industry terms, the same three experiments contribute an open,
reproducible graph substrate; an auditable early theme signal with risk
and compliance extensions; and a calibration of what edge-level
prediction can and cannot deliver. The Impact Statement states these
contributions per experiment.

\section{Financial Knowledge Graphs and LLM-Based Construction}
A knowledge graph encodes entities and the typed relationships between them.
Formally, a knowledge graph is a triple $G = (\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{R},
\mathcal{F})$, where $\mathcal{E}$ is the entity set, $\mathcal{R}$ the
relation set, and $\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{E} \times \mathcal{R}
\times \mathcal{E}$ a set of facts $(s, r, o)$ read as subject, relation,
object. A temporal, or dynamic, knowledge graph additionally records the
time at which each relationship holds: facts become quadruples
$(s, r, o, t)$, and the graph observable at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
G_t = (\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{R}, \mathcal{F}_t), \qquad
\mathcal{F}_t = \{ (s_i, r_i, o_i, t_i) : t_i < t \},
\label{eq:dkg}
\end{equation}
so that the graph can be reconstructed at any past instant and its
evolution studied over time. Every point-in-time constraint is a statement about Equation~\ref{eq:dkg}: a quantity
computed at week $t$ may depend on $\mathcal{F}_t$ only. This temporal
property is central because we are not concerned with whether a relationship
exists but with how quickly relationships form. Figure~\ref{fig:kgexample} shows a
fragment of such a graph in the ontology used throughout this work.

The most directly relevant system in the financial domain is FinDKG, introduced
by \citet{Li2024FinDKG}. FinDKG defines a fixed ontology of twelve entity types
and fifteen relation types, together with a standard temporal knowledge-graph
file format comprising entity and relation index files, a time index, and
chronologically split lists of quadruples. The
authors also release the Integrated Contextual Knowledge Graph (ICKG) relation
extractor, an open large-language-model extractor fine-tuned for the task of
knowledge-graph construction. This research adopts the FinDKG ontology and
on-disk format, but it builds its own graph with the ICKG extractor rather than
consuming the released FinDKG graph, so that the choice of corpus, the time
resolution, and the point-in-time discipline all remain under the author's
control.

ICKG is produced by
knowledge distillation: a small set of open financial news articles
(5{,}000 in the published pipeline) is labelled by GPT-4 under an
ontology-constrained extraction prompt, quality-filtered, and used to
fine-tune an open base model \citep{Li2024FinDKG}. The paper documents
version 3.2 on a Mistral-7B base; the later v4.2 release employed here
is the same extractor line rebuilt on the larger instruction-tuned
Qwen-2.5 base. Distillation from a frontier model is what makes the
extractor open and locally hostable, but it also means the extractor
inherits GPT-4's knowledge of events, and that residual hindsight
channel is measured directly in Section~\ref{ch:pit} rather than assumed
away.
The FinDKG authors analyse
their graph with an attention-based temporal network, KGTransformer,
which injects entity-type information into the attention mechanism, and
they detect trends by tracking the graph centrality of a named
entity through rolling snapshot graphs, demonstrated on COVID-19 and on
an AI-themed portfolio. The theme layer of Chapter~\ref{ch:exp2} asks
the converse question: surfacing emerging themes without naming
them in advance, as groups of co-accelerating entities, under
walk-forward discipline and with lead-time validation against a frozen
event list and a placebo control rather than descriptive inspection.

The
ICKG release employed here is a Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) adapter, in the sense
of \citet{Hu2022LoRA}, applied to the instruction-tuned Qwen-2.5 model
\citep{Qwen2024}. LoRA fine-tunes a small set of injected low-rank matrices while
freezing the base weights, which makes a fixed and fully documented extractor
practical to host locally. A local, fixed extractor is preferred to a frontier
model accessed through a commercial application-programming interface (API)
precisely because its knowledge cutoff is controllable and documented, a property
that matters for the point-in-time argument developed in Section~\ref{ch:pit}.
High-throughput extraction over the corpus relies on vLLM \citep{Kwon2023vLLM},
whose PagedAttention memory manager enables efficient batched generation on the
available hardware. The corpus itself is FNSPID \citep{Dong2024FNSPID}, which
aligns roughly 15.7 million timestamped financial-news records with stock-price
series for the S\&P~500 universe over the period 1999 to 2023; of these, the
present work uses only the full-text news component.

\section{Knowledge-Graph Embeddings and Link-Prediction Baselines}
\label{sec:kgembed}
The strongest non-temporal baselines for predicting which links a knowledge graph will contain
originate in the static knowledge-graph-embedding literature. The translational
model TransE, proposed by \citet{Bordes2013TransE}, represents a relation as a
translation between the vector embeddings of its subject and object entities,
scoring a candidate triple by
\begin{equation}
\phi_{\mathrm{TransE}}(s, r, o) = -\lVert \mathbf{e}_s + \mathbf{w}_r -
\mathbf{e}_o \rVert,
\label{eq:transe}
\end{equation}
so that a true triple's object embedding lies close to its subject's
embedding translated by the relation vector. Every embedding model in
this dissertation shares the template this established: a scoring
function over candidate triples, trained so that observed triples
outscore corrupted ones, with prediction by ranking every candidate
entity under the score. As
important as the model itself is the evaluation protocol
that accompanied it: the filtered entity-ranking procedure, reported through Mean
Reciprocal Rank (MRR) and Hits@$k$ over corrupted candidate entities, which has
since become the standard means of assessing link prediction and is adopted
throughout in its unfiltered
form, so that no evaluation-time knowledge of other true future facts enters a
prediction; this deflates absolute scores relative to the filtered convention
and is the stricter choice under point-in-time discipline. Two later models refine the scoring function.
DistMult \citep{Yang2015DistMult} scores a triple with a bilinear diagonal form,
and ComplEx \citep{Trouillon2016ComplEx} extends this idea to complex-valued
embeddings, whose Hermitian product is able to represent both symmetric and
antisymmetric relations. Their scoring functions are
\begin{equation}
\phi_{\mathrm{DistMult}}(s, r, o) = \langle \mathbf{e}_s, \mathbf{w}_r,
\mathbf{e}_o \rangle, \qquad
\phi_{\mathrm{ComplEx}}(s, r, o) = \operatorname{Re} \langle \mathbf{e}_s,
\mathbf{w}_r, \overline{\mathbf{e}_o} \rangle,
\label{eq:scoring}
\end{equation}
with real embeddings in the first case and complex in the second; the
conjugation is what lets ComplEx represent asymmetric relations. Because these models score the structural plausibility
of a triple independently of whether it has recurred in the past, they are
informative baselines on novel edges, and it is in this role
that they are used in Experiment~3.

\section{Temporal Knowledge Graph Forecasting}
Forecasting on a temporal knowledge graph, also called the extrapolation setting,
requires that only history strictly preceding the query time may be used to
predict future links. This setting has been dominated by recurrent neural
architectures. RE-NET, introduced by \citet{Jin2020RENET}, infers graph structure
autoregressively, generating the events of each timestep conditioned on those of
the preceding ones. RE-GCN, due to \citet{Li2021REGCN}, learns time-evolving
representations of entities and relations by passing each snapshot of the graph
through a relational graph convolutional network and folding the result into a
recurrent state, with a decoder that scores candidate future edges. The temporal
graph neural network implemented here follows the RE-GCN design.
The FinDKG authors' KGTransformer belongs to the same evolution-based
family, adding entity-type information to the attention mechanism
\citep{Li2024FinDKG}; the benchmark of Experiment~3 instead pairs the
widely replicated RE-GCN design with the recurrence critique below,
because the question asked there is whether temporal structure helps at
all under point-in-time discipline, not which temporal architecture is
strongest.

A recent and directly relevant critique tempers expectations of these models.
\citet{Gastinger2024Recurrence} show that a simple, training-free recurrence
baseline, which scores a candidate fact according to how often that exact fact
has occurred in the past,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{rec}(s, r, o, t) = \bigl| \{ t' < t : (s, r, o, t') \in
\mathcal{F} \} \bigr|,
\label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}
rivals or outperforms many neural temporal
knowledge-graph forecasters, and they argue that part of the apparent progress in
the field is an artefact of comparison against weak baselines. This finding
directly motivates the baselining adopted in Experiment~3, in which the
recurrence baseline is treated as a competitor rather than a
formality. It
also anticipates that the temporal
graph neural network adds little forecasting advantage over static
structure on novel edges.

\section{Classical Link-Prediction Heuristics}
Before the embedding and neural methods described above, link prediction was
studied as a purely structural problem in the social-network literature.
\citet{LibenNowell2007} surveyed a family of proximity measures for predicting
which links would later appear in a network, the simplest of which counts the
neighbours that two nodes already share. The index of \citet{AdamicAdar2003}
refines this count by summing over the shared neighbours of a candidate pair,
weighting each by the inverse logarithm of its degree so that rarer common
neighbours count for more. For a candidate pair $(x, y)$ with
neighbourhoods $N(\cdot)$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CN}(x, y) = |N(x) \cap N(y)|, \qquad
\mathrm{AA}(x, y) = \sum_{z \in N(x) \cap N(y)} \frac{1}{\log |N(z)|}.
\label{eq:heuristics}
\end{equation} These heuristics encode assumptions of triadic closure
and homophily that hold well in social graphs. Whether the same assumptions
transfer to a financial co-mention graph is an empirical question rather than a
settled one, and a negative answer would itself be informative; for this reason
the Common Neighbours and Adamic--Adar heuristics are retained as baselines in
Experiment~3.

\section{Burst and Event Detection in Streams}
Detecting the moment at which a relationship or a theme suddenly intensifies is a
burst-detection problem. The standard approach is the model of
\citet{Kleinberg2003Bursty}, which represents the stream as the output of an
automaton whose states correspond to different rates of arrival. In its simplest,
two-state form, the optimal state sequence is recovered by trading the improved
likelihood of attributing a stretch of high activity to an elevated-rate state
against a cost incurred each time the automaton switches into that state, so that
only sustained runs of high activity are reported as bursts. Formally
the stream's weekly counts $c_t$ are modelled as
\begin{equation}
c_t \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_{q_t}), \qquad \lambda_0 = N/T, \quad
\lambda_1 = s \lambda_0,
\label{eq:burstmodel}
\end{equation}
with $q_t$ the automaton's state at week $t$, $N/T$ the mean arrival
rate over the span, and $s > 1$ the elevated-rate multiplier: a burst is
a maximum-likelihood attribution of a stretch of counts to the elevated
state, priced against the cost of entering it. The two-state model
tends to merge distinct but adjacent episodes into a single long burst, a
limitation that the hierarchical variant addresses by separating them according
to intensity.

Kleinberg's automaton is one of several treatments of the problem. A complementary and
much older tradition treats the same question as sequential change
detection: the cumulative-sum (CUSUM) control chart of \citet{Page1954CUSUM}
accumulates standardised deviations from a baseline rate and raises an alarm
when the cumulative excess crosses a decision interval, so that it responds to
sustained shifts in level rather than to discrete spikes. A third treatment is fully Bayesian:
the Bayesian online changepoint detection of \citet{AdamsMacKay2007BOCD}
maintains an exact online posterior over the time since the last changepoint
(here with a conjugate Gamma--Poisson model; changepoints are back-dated to
the inferred onset).
We run all three detectors over the edge-formation series, so
that detected episodes are demonstrably not an artefact of a single algorithm:
empirically they agree on where activity is elevated while segmenting it at
different granularities (Kleinberg isolating discrete events, CUSUM and BOCD
sustained regimes). Since interval widths differ, recall is judged against
each detector's own chance level (its dilated flagged-week coverage, that is,
the recall it would achieve on random event weeks): on the gold events recall runs
78--94\% against chance of 16--29\% (an excess of +59 to +66 points), with a
three-detector union of 18/18.

Detecting that individual entities accelerate is, however, not yet theme
discovery: an emerging theme is a group of related entities accelerating
together. For this step, we cluster co-accelerating entities on
their recent co-occurrence subgraph using the Louvain community-detection
algorithm of \citet{Blondel2008Louvain}, a fast greedy optimiser of modularity,
and track the resulting communities week over week into theme lifelines,
a construction in the spirit of the dynamic-community-tracking literature.
The resulting event catalogue and theme lifelines are validated against an
independently compiled list of known episodes.

\section{Point-in-Time Integrity and Lookahead Bias}
The central methodological risk in any financial study that relies on a model
trained on data from after the prediction date is lookahead bias, that is, the
leakage of future information into a prediction that is supposed to depend only on
the past. The risk is acute here because the extractor used to build the graph is
a pretrained language model whose knowledge cutoff postdates much of the corpus,
so the resulting hindsight must be measured rather than assumed away. The
most applicable work is that of \citet{He2025Chrono}, who train chronologically
consistent language models, including ChronoBERT, exclusively on text available
up to a stated date, so that such models are free of lookahead bias by
construction. Comparing two versions of the same model that differ only in their
training cutoff yields a capacity-controlled estimate of the bias, which the
authors report to be modest. The estimand is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{lookahead}} = M\bigl(f_{c_2}\bigr) - M\bigl(f_{c_1}\bigr),
\qquad c_1 < c_2,
\label{eq:lookaheadgap}
\end{equation}
where $f_c$ is the same architecture trained only on text available up
to cutoff $c$ and $M$ evaluates both models on identical queries: any
systematic advantage of the later cutoff on events predating $c_1$ is
attributable to knowledge of the future. Direct measurements in financial
applications are consistent with this picture: \citet{GlassermanLin2023} compare
GPT-based sentiment strategies on original against anonymised headlines
and find that, while look-ahead bias is detectable within the model's
training window, it is dominated by a distraction effect and is not a
concern out of sample; Section~\ref{ch:pit} measures the same channel
for entity-level extraction with a cutoff-controlled instrument. These models descend architecturally from BERT
\citep{Devlin2019BERT}, the bidirectional transformer encoder. Two alternatives
sometimes suggested for time-aware financial language modelling are
distinguished here. FinBERT \citep{Araci2019FinBERT}, a sentiment-oriented
BERT fine-tune, applies no chronological control to its training text and is
therefore itself exposed to the lookahead risk under study, which is why the
chronologically consistent family is preferred here.
Similarly, the sinusoidal positional encodings of the Transformer \citep{Vaswani2017Attention}
encode token position within a sequence rather than calendar-time information
availability; point-in-time correctness is accordingly enforced here at the
data and evaluation-protocol level, where it is verifiable, rather than inside
the encoder. Experiment~3 uses
ChronoBERT entity embeddings in two ways: as a point-in-time-clean semantic
baseline in its own right, and, through the cutoff-controlled comparison, as the
instrument for the lookahead-bias diagnostic developed in Section~\ref{ch:pit}.
Throughout the dissertation, uncertainty in the reported metrics is quantified
using the bootstrap \citep{Efron1979Bootstrap}, which resamples the test
instances with replacement in order to estimate the sampling distribution of a
statistic without recourse to parametric assumptions.

\section{Data: the FNSPID Corpus}
FNSPID \citep{Dong2024FNSPID} aligns roughly 15.7 million timestamped
financial-news records with stock-price series for the S\&P~500 universe; only
the full-text news component is used here. The study window is 2017--2023 (84
months, 365 weekly buckets), chosen to cover the COVID-19 shock and the
generative-AI surge together with a multi-year pre-episode baseline.

Raw article counts in FNSPID are strongly skewed towards recent years, so a
graph built from a uniform random sample would confound structural change with
sampling density. Subsets are therefore time-stratified: a single
streaming pass over the corpus performs per-month reservoir sampling
\citep{Vitter1985Reservoir} with a fixed seed, keeping only articles whose
bodies exceed 200 words. Two scales are drawn: 2{,}400 articles per month
($=201{,}600$) and 7{,}200 per month ($=604{,}800$). Even monthly coverage
guarantees that week-on-week changes in the graph reflect changes in reported
structure rather than changes in corpus volume.

\section{Extraction with ICKG-v4.2}
Each article is passed to ICKG-v4.2, the FinDKG relation extractor
\citep{Li2024FinDKG}, hosted locally via vLLM \citep{Kwon2023vLLM} with greedy
decoding and the verbatim FinDKG prompt. The extractor arrives
distilled: its instruction data were generated by GPT-4 over a small
article set and quality-filtered before fine-tuning
(Chapter~\ref{ch:background}), which is why extractor hindsight is
treated in Section~\ref{ch:pit} as a channel to be measured. The extractor returns typed triples
over the fixed ontology of twelve entity types and fifteen relation types,
with themes realised as CONCEPT and EVENT nodes.

A practical engineering finding: the model emits triples in two formats
interchangeably, JSON arrays of arrays ($\approx$65\% of articles) and
Python-style parenthesised tuples ($\approx$35\%), and a parser tolerant of
both (plus a filter for purely numeric ``entities'') raised the usable-output
rate in the pilot from 35\% to over 99\%. The full runs achieved 0\% malformed
generations at both scales, $\sim$18.5 valid triples per article, and 11.5
million raw triples at the larger scale. A precision spot-check found both
entities of a triple verbatim in the source text for 83\% of a sampled set,
a lower bound, since the extractor frequently simplifies surface forms.

\section{Edge Construction and Publication-Date Timestamping}
Every edge is timestamped by the article's publication date, never by
the date of any event the text describes; this is the single most important
leakage control in the pipeline. Triples are aggregated into FinDKG-format
quadruples with weekly time-bucketing, where the weight of an edge-week is the
number of supporting mentions in that week. Chronological splits are fixed
once, by week: train 0--254, validation 255--309, test 310--364 (a 70/15/15
division of the 365 weeks).

\section{Compute and Throughput}
Extraction ran on a UCL cluster node with two RTX Pro 6000 GPUs, one vLLM
instance per GPU over interleaved article shards, taking $\sim$14\,h for the
200k corpus and $\sim$43\,h for the 600k corpus, with CSV-append checkpointing
so that interrupted runs resume without repeated work. Every subsequent
analysis in this dissertation, including all of Chapters~\ref{ch:exp2} and
\ref{ch:exp3}, runs on a single consumer GPU with 6\,GB of memory, which is
a deliberate reproducibility property of the design.

\section{The Syndication-Duplication Audit and the Deduplicated Core}
FNSPID stores one row per article--ticker pair, so an article syndicated to
$k$ tickers can enter a subset up to $k$ times verbatim. Hashing article
bodies reveals that \textbf{10.5\%} of the 200k subset and \textbf{24.3\%} of
the 600k subset are exact duplicates; of 102{,}426 duplicate groups in the
larger subset, 101{,}346 differ only in the ticker column, and only 476 span
more than one week. The damage is therefore concentrated in same-week
weight inflation: a threshold such as ``keep edges supported by at least
three mentions'' can be satisfied by a single syndicated article, so
duplication silently biases which edges enter a denoised core.

Removing duplicates (keeping one copy per body) and rebuilding with unchanged
thresholds halves the denoised 600k core, from 41{,}888 to 21{,}216 entities
(212{,}294 edge-weeks). This deduplicated core is the only
construction whose thresholds mean what they claim, namely support by $N$
independent articles, and it is adopted as the canonical graph for the
remainder of the dissertation. All principal conclusions are re-verified on
it, and the audit itself is reported as a finding that applies to any
per-ticker news corpus.

\section{Extraction-Noise Controls and Limitations}
Noise is measured rather than hidden. In the full graphs roughly two thirds of
entities are singletons, motivating the degree/weight cores. Templated
financial-media boilerplate is measured with the slot-fingerprint method of
Section~\ref{sec:noisecontrols} and removed by an opt-in filter (on the
canonical core: 291 entities carrying 15.6\% of edge-instances). Only light
surface-form normalisation is applied; entity resolution is deliberately out
of scope (FinDKG itself normalises mentions with Sentence-BERT-based
disambiguation, and resolution is the subject of a parallel project on
this corpus), but a crude corporate-suffix merge bounds its effect: test
novelty falls from 72.4\% to 68.4\% (200k) and from 64.0\% to 60.1\% (600k),
so fragmentation is real but secondary. Country (GPE) relations are sparse
owing to the US-centric corpus and are documented as a limitation rather than
hand-curated.

\section{Results and Discussion}
The substrate meets its three design goals. It is reproducible: fixed
seeds, checkpointed extraction, and a fully scripted path from raw corpus to
splits. It is point-in-time clean by construction at the graph level:
publication-date timestamps and chronological splits are enforced before any
model sees the data. Finally, it showcases its noise: duplication,
fragmentation and boilerplate are quantified, with the deduplicated core as
the corrected canonical object. Scale behaves as hoped, in that tripling the corpus deepens evidence per
relationship (test novelty falls monotonically across the constructions in
Table~\ref{tab:constructions}), and the duplication audit stands as a transferable methodological result about
building graphs from per-ticker news data.

\section{Introduction}
This chapter contains the dissertation's central contribution: a walk-forward
system that discovers emerging themes as they form. The pipeline has four
stages: score each entity's formation acceleration causally, detect bursts,
cluster co-accelerating entities on the graph into themes, and track themes
into lifelines whose birth week is the date the system would have flagged them
live. The pipeline is then validated against a frozen gold list with a placebo
control. All analyses run on the canonical deduplicated core of
Chapter~\ref{ch:exp1}.

The motivation of this experiment is threefold: first, emerging themes are
usually recognised only once they are widely reported, so a graph-native
early signal has practical value; second, existing burst-detection work
operates on term frequencies rather than on relationship formation, leaving
the structural signal unexplored; and third, a detection claim is credible
only under strict walk-forward discipline with placebo-controlled validation,
which this experiment provides.

\section{Velocity and Acceleration: Definitions}
A formation is the first week in which a distinct
(subject, relation, object) triple appears. For any entity, relation or theme,
let $c_t$ denote its formation count in week $t$. Velocity and acceleration
are the first and second differences,
\begin{equation}
v_t = \Delta c_t = c_t - c_{t-1}, \qquad
a_t = \Delta^2 c_t = v_t - v_{t-1},
\label{eq:veldef}
\end{equation}
per node, per relation and per theme. Two window conventions serve two
purposes: centred windows for descriptive reporting, where a peak
should align with the event that caused it, and strictly trailing
windows for the detection system, which must be causal to claim real-time
operation.

The raw second difference in Equation~\ref{eq:veldef} is an unbiased but
extremely noisy acceleration estimate for sparse count series. The detector
therefore uses its windowed, variance-stabilised form: the surprise
statistic of the next section, which contrasts the recent-window formation
rate with the entity's own baseline rate. This is the operational form of
``anomalous acceleration'' throughout the dissertation: a smoothed estimate of
the velocity change, robust to the count noise that dominates $\Delta^2 c_t$.

\section{Causal Acceleration Scoring}
\label{sec:causalaccel}
At week $t$, with a recent window of $r=4$ weeks and a baseline window of
$b=26$ weeks, define for every entity
\begin{equation}
O_t = \sum_{s=t-r+1}^{t} c_s, \qquad
E_t = \frac{r}{b} \sum_{s=t-r-b+1}^{t-r} c_s, \qquad
z_t = \frac{O_t - E_t}{\sqrt{E_t + 1}} .
\label{eq:surprise}
\end{equation}
Both windows are strictly trailing. Under a Poisson null the denominator
stabilises the variance; the $+1$ floor keeps $z_t$ finite for zero baselines,
so brand-new entities (``Coronavirus'' in January 2020) score high by design;
new-entity emergence is a feature, not an artefact.
Figure~\ref{fig:surprise} traces the statistic through the flagship
episode.

Weekly formation counts are in fact strongly over-dispersed. A
negative-binomial variant replaces the denominator with
$\sqrt{E_t + \alpha E_t^2 + 1}$, where the dispersion $\alpha = 3.75$ is
estimated once by the method of moments on training weeks only:
per entity $i$ with training-week mean $m_i$ and variance $s_i^2$,
\begin{equation}
\alpha_i = \frac{s_i^2 - m_i}{m_i^2}, \qquad
\alpha = \operatorname{median}\{ \alpha_i : m_i \geq 0.25 \} = 3.75,
\label{eq:nbmom}
\end{equation}
the median giving robustness to the heavy upper tail of per-entity
dispersion estimates. Raising the
variance raises the detection bar: the Poisson floor is the high-recall
configuration and the NB correction the high-precision one, and the gold-list
conclusion of Section~\ref{sec:themevalidation} holds under both. Two
disclosures apply: $\alpha$ is estimated on weeks 0--254 but applied at every
$t$, which is conservative for earlier events; and the template blacklist of
Section~\ref{sec:noisecontrols} is a static full-period list. Together these
are the only non-trailing inputs to an otherwise walk-forward system.

\section{Burst and Change-Point Methods}
Three detectors with different inductive biases are run on the formation
series, so that detected episodes are demonstrably not an artefact of one
algorithm. Kleinberg's two-state automaton \citep{Kleinberg2003Bursty} finds
discrete bursts by a Viterbi trade-off between emission likelihood and a
transition cost for entering the elevated state. Formally, for a state
sequence $q$ over $T$ weeks with baseline rate $\lambda_0 = N/T$ (total
events over span) and elevated rate $\lambda_1 = s\lambda_0$ with $s = 2$,
the automaton minimises
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{cost}(q) = \sum_t \left[ \lambda_{q_t} - c_t \ln \lambda_{q_t} \right]
+ \gamma \ln(T) \cdot \#\{t : q_t = 1,\, q_{t-1} = 0\},
\label{eq:kleinberg}
\end{equation}
whose first term is the Poisson negative log-likelihood of the weekly
counts $c_t$ up to a constant and whose second prices each entry into the
elevated state; bursts are the maximal $q_t = 1$ runs of the minimising
sequence. The one-sided CUSUM chart
\citep{Page1954CUSUM} accumulates standardised deviations,
$C_t = \max(0,\, C_{t-1} + z_t - k)$ with reference value $k=0.5$, and alarms
when $C_t$ exceeds a decision interval $h=5$, responding to sustained shifts.
Bayesian online changepoint detection \citep{AdamsMacKay2007BOCD} maintains
the exact run-length posterior under a Gamma--Poisson model with hazard
$1/100$; changepoints are back-dated to the inferred onset $t - \ell_t$, where
$\ell_t$ is the MAP run length. Writing $\pi_{t-1}(r)$ for
$P(r_{t-1} = r \mid x_{1:t-1})$, the run-length posterior obeys the exact
online recursion
\begin{equation}
P(r_t \mid x_{1:t}) \propto P(x_t \mid r_t)\,\bigl[ (1-h)\, \pi_{t-1}(r_t - 1)
+ \mathbf{1}\{r_t = 0\}\, h \textstyle\sum_{r'} \pi_{t-1}(r') \bigr],
\label{eq:bocd}
\end{equation}
where the first bracket term continues the current run and the second
starts a new one; Gamma--Poisson conjugacy gives the predictive
$P(x_t \mid r_t)$ in closed form as a negative binomial, which is what
makes the posterior exact rather than approximate.

The three detectors flag intervals of very different widths: Kleinberg
isolates events, while CUSUM and BOCD mark sustained regimes (their global
intervals agree at Jaccard 0.81). Recall must therefore be judged against each detector's own
chance level, defined as its dilated flagged-week coverage: the recall it
would achieve on random event weeks. On an 18-event gold list of documented
2017--2023 episodes (Table~\ref{tab:detectors}), all three detect far above
chance with comparable excesses, and their union recovers every event.
These detectors are retrospective descriptive tools (their baselines use the
full series); the walk-forward system is the theme detector itself.

\section{Theme Clustering and Lifelines}
Individual acceleration is not yet a theme: an emerging theme is a group of
related entities accelerating together. Each week, entities passing the gate
($z_t \geq 3$ and $O_t \geq 6$) are placed on their trailing eight-week
co-occurrence subgraph and clustered with Louvain
\citep{Blondel2008Louvain} (fixed seed; clusters of fewer than three members
discarded). Louvain greedily maximises the modularity
\begin{equation}
Q = \frac{1}{2m} \sum_{ij} \left[ A_{ij} - \frac{k_i k_j}{2m} \right]
\delta(c_i, c_j),
\label{eq:modularity}
\end{equation}
where $A$ holds the trailing eight-week co-mention weights, $k_i$ the
weighted degrees, $m$ the total edge weight and $\delta$ selects
same-community pairs: a community scores well exactly when its internal
weight exceeds the degree-preserving chance expectation. Clusters are ranked by total excess formations
$\sum_i (O_t - E_t)_i$ and the top eight per week retained. Themes are
tracked week-over-week into lifelines by member overlap (Jaccard
$\geq 0.3$), matching live lifelines first (a continuation beats a
re-activation regardless of overlap) and allowing a lifeline to be claimed
by at most one cluster per week; a re-activation after a gap of more than
eight weeks rejoins its old lifeline rather than counting as a new emergence.
The matching statistic and the ranking score are
\begin{equation}
J(A, B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|} \geq 0.3, \qquad
S(C) = \sum_{i \in C} (O_i - E_i),
\label{eq:lifeline}
\end{equation}
the member-set overlap deciding continuity and the total excess deciding
rank. A lifeline's birth week is the date the system would have flagged the
theme live. Algorithm~\ref{alg:detector} assembles the full weekly loop
in one place.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{One week of the walk-forward theme detector. At week $t$ every
quantity is computed from history $\leq t$ only; lifeline updates happen
after matching, preserving point-in-time discipline.}
\label{alg:detector}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require formation counts for weeks $\leq t$; live lifeline set
$\mathcal{L}$; template blacklist
\For{each entity $i$ not template-excluded}
  \State $O_i \gets$ formations in the trailing 4 weeks;
  $E_i \gets$ scaled 26-week baseline
  \State $z_i \gets (O_i - E_i)/\sqrt{E_i + 1}$
  \Comment{NB variant: $\sqrt{E_i + \alpha E_i^2 + 1}$}
\EndFor
\State $A \gets \{i : z_i \geq 3 \text{ and } O_i \geq 6\}$
\Comment{accelerating set}
\State $G \gets$ trailing 8-week co-occurrence subgraph induced on $A$
\State $\mathcal{C} \gets$ Louvain$(G)$; discard $|C| < 3$; keep the top 8
clusters by $\sum_{i \in C}(O_i - E_i)$
\For{each cluster $C \in \mathcal{C}$ in rank order}
  \State match $C$ to an unclaimed lifeline $L \in \mathcal{L}$ with
  $J(C, L) \geq 0.3$, live lifelines first
  \State \textbf{if} no match \textbf{then} open a new lifeline with birth
  week $t$
  \State label EMERGING if the excess-weighted share of members with fewer
  than two prior episodes is high
\EndFor
\State update the habituation book-keeping with this week's accelerating
entities \Comment{after matching}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Data-Driven Noise Controls}
\label{sec:noisecontrols}
Two failure modes require control, and both controls are measured from data
rather than hand-curated. First, templated screener articles (options
screens, quantitative stock reports) inject formulaic entities whose ramp-ups
mimic themes. Slot-normalised fingerprints of titles and body openings, in which rare
tokens are replaced by a placeholder so that ``company-name slots'' collapse,
identify 26.4\% of articles as template-generated; the 2{,}010 entities
drawing $\geq 60\%$ of their mentions from such articles are excluded from
candidacy. Second, recurring rituals (earnings-season screens, index
complexes) accelerate every quarter without being new. A habituation
score classifies a newborn lifeline as EMERGING only if the excess-weighted
share of members with fewer than two prior acceleration episodes is at least
one half, so a theme is emerging only when the entities driving it are new
phenomena. A stricter configuration additionally excludes walk-forward
ubiquitous entities (active in more than half of all past weeks); it
dissolves star-shaped screener clusters at some cost in lead time, and both
configurations are reported.

and longer slightly earlier (full grid in Appendix~B); and the
negative-binomial configuration detects 7/10 at a chance rate of $0.9/10$.

\section{Lifeline-Level Precision Audit}
\label{sec:precisionaudit}
The placebo control establishes precision at the week level; it does not say
what share of all flagged lifelines are real storylines. We therefore
audit every EMERGING lifeline directly. For each lifeline an audit pack is
assembled from the provenance chain: its top twelve members by accumulated
excess, its birth month, and up to ten source headlines from the birth
window, the top three carrying the article's opening text. Packs contain no
detector scores, so no judge can defer to the system's own confidence. Each
pack is classified under a rubric frozen before any judging: REAL (a
coherent, externally documentable storyline with genuine coverage),
TEMPLATE (screener or boilerplate constructs dominate) or
INCOHERENT (incidental co-occurrence). The judge is a locally hosted
quantised Qwen2.5-14B language model under greedy decoding with a fixed
prompt, an LLM-as-judge design \citep{Zheng2023Judge}, and its labels are
quoted only because it passes two validations:
eleven of eleven control themes are labelled correctly (eight earnings-ritual
themes as TEMPLATE, three COVID lifelines as REAL), and on a seed-fixed
random subset of fourteen packs hand-labelled by the author it agrees on
13/14 (93\%) of the binary real-versus-noise decisions the estimate uses,
with the three-way disagreements confined to the TEMPLATE/INCOHERENT
boundary and the model marginally the more conservative rater. A further
ten hand-labelled packs from the template-filtered arm serve as a transfer
check (8/10 binary agreement; 21/24 across both subsets, Cohen's
$\kappa = 0.74$); both transfer
disagreements are cases where the judge reads a single storyline inside a
cluster the author assessed as over-merged, so the template-filtered
estimate is, if anything, marginally optimistic. Precision is quoted
throughout with Wilson score intervals \citep{Wilson1927}: for $k$ REAL
among $n$ packs and
$p = k/n$, the 95\% interval is
\begin{equation}
\frac{p + z^2/2n}{1 + z^2/n} \;\pm\; \frac{z}{1 + z^2/n}
\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n} + \frac{z^2}{4n^2}}, \qquad z = 1.96,
\label{eq:wilson}
\end{equation}
preferred over the Wald interval for its coverage at moderate $n$ and
proportions away from one half.

Three findings follow (Table~\ref{tab:precaudit} and
Figure~\ref{fig:auditfrontier}). First, the
high-recall stream is roughly one-third real: 37.9\% of lenient lifelines
are genuine storylines, and ranking does not rescue the reader, since
screener rituals generate large excess counts and rank high (top-25 by peak
score: 28\% REAL). The largest correctable share of the failure mass is
exactly the boilerplate the noise controls target: the entity-level template filter removes
template-dominated entities, but clusters of legitimate entities can
still be bound together by template articles, as when an options
screener fires across many tickers in one week. Second, structural
cohesion cannot certify quality. Template themes bind more densely than
real ones (median share of binding articles mentioning three or more members
0.121 against 0.105), because a mail-merge article lists many concepts at
once; what distinguishes real themes is mutual pair connectivity (median
pair coverage 0.643 against 0.434). The best post-hoc structural filter
(pair coverage $\geq 0.5$) lifts precision to 47.4\% while retaining 68.2\%
of real themes, and stacked filters saturate near 50\%: the residue is
semantic, not structural. Third, the audit points to its own remedy.
Excluding template-fingerprinted articles (25.1\% of the corpus, a
re-implemented and documented fingerprinter) from the detector substrate
removes 16.9\% of triple instances and yields a configuration that keeps
every gold-list detection (8/10, median $-1$ week, placebo
$p = 5\times10^{-5}$) and essentially every real discovery (109 REAL against
107) while shrinking the stream by 13\% and cutting template lifelines from
86 to 54: precision rises to 44.7\%, and conservatively so, since the
fingerprinter under-catches skeletons that embed full company names. One
result cuts the other way and is reported as such: the negative-binomial
configuration, whose event-level specificity is the strongest (chance
$0.9/10$), has the highest template share of any arm (44\%); tail
tightening suppresses ordinary noise but not boilerplate, whose counts are
large and systematic, so event-detection specificity and stream purity are
distinct properties that must be measured separately. The audit also
supplied a concrete specimen of the deduplication bound stated in
Chapter~\ref{ch:exp1}: one syndicated article appears three times, twice
byte-identical (and deduplicated) and once differing by a single character,
which survives exact hashing. Manual inspection additionally decomposes the
INCOHERENT mass: part of it is not absent signal but over-merged
clusters containing several distinct storylines (in one specimen an
AstraZeneca and a BHP storyline fused, plausibly through index-roundup
co-mentions), a clustering-granularity failure that member counts do not
separate from incidental bags. A direct test of the obvious remedy fails:
raising the Louvain resolution to 1.5 on the template-filtered substrate
keeps the gold list (8/10, median $-1$ week) and recovers some split
storylines (117 REAL lifelines against 109), but rituals and bags fragment
into more units still (284 lifelines, 78 TEMPLATE, 89 INCOHERENT; precision
41.2\%), and resolution 2.0 loses a gold-list detection. The over-merge
failure mode therefore calls for drift-aware lifeline tracking or locally
adaptive splitting rather than a uniform resolution increase; both remain
future work. Scope: the judge assesses whether sourcing is
genuine and related, not whether the reported news is true, and the audit
rests on one judging model under one rubric, anchored by the human subset
and controls. Two candidate second judges (a 7B and a quantised 14B of
different model families) were run on the identical packs and failed
the control gate, labelling nearly every pack REAL including all
earnings-ritual controls; both were excluded, an outcome consistent
with the gate's design: the validation is carried by the controls and the human subset,
not by model consensus.

\textbf{A distilled lightweight filter.} The audit's labels make a final
reduction in inference cost: a logistic regression on twelve cheap, text-free pack
features (detector outputs at birth, the cohesion statistics, member-type
composition, and the binding articles' template share) distils the judge
into a standing filter with no language model at inference, under a
protocol fixed before fitting (grouped cross-validation by birth year;
fixed comparison points; hypothesis stated in advance). It reaches PR-AUC
0.602 against a 0.379 base rate, and tuned to retain half the real themes
it lifts stream precision from 37.9\% to 68.4\%; at the 68\% retention of
the fixed threshold baseline it edges that baseline (50.7\% against
47.4\%), reconfirming from a third direction that text-free filtering
saturates near 50\% at high retention. The standardised coefficients turn
the audit's qualitative findings into effect sizes: the concept share of
the membership is the strongest negative signal ($-0.71$; real themes are
actor-heavy) and mutual pair connectivity the strongest positive ($+0.67$),
while freshness is mildly negative, consistent with the earlier observation
that template clusters are often ``fresh''. Two limits are disclosed:
agreement with the hand-labelled subset is 79\%, below the judge's 93\%,
and a strict temporal holdout (training before 2022, testing on the 46
later lifelines, whose REAL rate falls to 21.7\%) retains a ranking lift
(PR-AUC 0.406 against 0.217) but not fixed-threshold precision, so the
filter inherits the regime dependence recorded among the limitations and
would need periodic re-distillation in deployment.

\textbf{A fine-tuned small judge.} The text-free filter's ceiling
motivates one further distillation, this time keeping a language model
but shrinking it. The teacher's labels across every audited
configuration, including the rejected resolution sweep (1{,}145 packs in
all), fine-tune a 1.5B-parameter judge by low-rank adaptation
\citep{Hu2022LoRA}, under a protocol fixed before training: packs are
grouped into storylines by cross-configuration member overlap so that
near-duplicates of one story never straddle the split, and the held-out
groups are additionally temporal, born 2022 or later. The result passes
exactly the validation gate that the zero-shot candidates failed. The base model fails
the eleven-control gate (it over-labels TEMPLATE, the opposite failure
mode to the second judges' uniform REAL labelling), while eight minutes of adapter
training on a consumer GPU yield a perfect gate, 9/9 noise controls to
TEMPLATE and 3/3 real to REAL, under the same greedy protocol. On the
held-out storyline groups the small judge agrees with its teacher on
82.6\% of binary REAL-versus-noise calls (69.1\% three-way, the
confusions being the same TEMPLATE-versus-INCOHERENT boundary the human
comparison found), and it matches the twenty-four hand-labelled packs
on 20/24, one short of the teacher itself (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.66$
against the teacher's $0.74$). Deployed over the full
stream it tracks the teacher's REAL share within three percentage
points in every configuration. The score behind the retention curves is
computed exactly rather than sampled:
\begin{equation}
P(\mathrm{REAL} \mid \mathrm{pack}) =
\frac{P(\ell_{\mathrm{REAL}} \mid \mathrm{prompt})}
{\sum_{\ell} P(\ell \mid \mathrm{prompt})}, \qquad
P(\ell \mid \mathrm{prompt}) = \prod_{k} P\bigl(w_k^{\ell} \mid
\mathrm{prompt}, w_{<k}^{\ell}\bigr),
\label{eq:judgescore}
\end{equation}
each candidate label's probability being the product of its
completion-token probabilities under the fine-tuned model, renormalised
over the three labels. Used as a ranking score it reaches held-out PR-AUC
0.766 against a 0.237 base rate (precision 0.565 at 30\% retention). The comparison with the text-free
filter is made paired and like-for-like: retrained on the matching
training side and scored on the same held-out packs (the 46 held-out
lenient lifelines), the filter reaches
0.406 where the judge reaches 0.756, an advantage of $+0.35$ PR-AUC
[paired bootstrap 95\% CI $+0.13, +0.55$];
Figure~\ref{fig:retention} shows the two retention curves.

The judge's probabilities are
also usable as probabilities, within a stated limit: the held-out Brier
score is 0.112 against an all-base-rate reference of 0.181 (five-bin
expected calibration error 0.074), with a monotone reliability curve
that is over-confident in the middle bins, where packs scored 0.4--0.6
are empirically 30\% REAL (the reliability curve is reproduced in

inference: a model roughly
an order of magnitude smaller reproduces the labels well enough to run
as a standing filter on every detector pass. It remains a distillation,
not an independent check; trained on the teacher's labels alone, it
inherits the teacher's blind spots by construction.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:exp2results}
The central empirical pattern is that lead time reflects the
information structure of the event itself. Anticipatable themes, those
whose build-up is reported before the market-moving moment, are detected
weeks early: the COVID market crisis three weeks before the crash, the
vaccine race eight weeks before Operation Warp Speed was announced, the
efficacy theme six weeks before the Pfizer readout. Pure shocks, such as an
invasion, a bank run or a strike, land at the news-flow floor of $+1$ week,
because no public reporting precedes them. The two misses are genuine rather
than algorithmic, and they fail for different reasons, dissected at the end
of this section: thin corpus coverage in one case, credit withheld by the
conservative birth-window rule in the other. GameStop is detected in
the squeeze
week but classified recurring, since squeeze-adjacent entities had
prior acceleration episodes; the label is a genuine reflection of the
habituation bookkeeping rather than an error. Beyond the gold list the system surfaces
correctly dated discoveries including the 2019 cannabis boom
(HEXO/Aurora/Tilray, born 2019-03-18), the EV/SPAC wave (Nikola/Nio, born
2020-06-22) and the stay-at-home complex (Zoom/Kroger/Virgin Galactic, born
2020-04-06).

\textbf{The COVID theme, week by week.} The flagship detection is
presented at weekly resolution, exactly as a live system would have
surfaced it.
In the week of 2020-02-03, three weeks before the market break of
2020-02-24, a new cluster entered the theme list in second place (score
57.5, seven members): the newly accelerating Coronavirus entity, bound to
consumer names with China exposure (Starbucks, Beyond Meat, Tesla) and the
drug maker Biogen. Contemporaneous coverage was still dismissive; one of
the theme's own binding articles that fortnight is headlined ``Dow Jones
Today: Stocks Shrug Off Q1 GDP Warning'' (2020-02-10). The lifeline
persisted through mid-February (2020-02-17, score 55.8, Global Markets and
Caterpillar joining) and in the crash week itself, 2020-02-24, moved to
first place (score 87.7, nine members, Morgan Stanley now among them). On
2020-03-02 it drew in the healthcare sector fund, and it then grew with the
crisis, ranked first again on 2020-03-09 (score 129.1) and 2020-03-16
(score 157.7, twelve members), by which point the membership read as a
cross-section of the pandemic economy: United Airlines, Plug Power, the
market indices. Two properties of the episode generalise. Detection
preceded the price event because the news-flow build-up did; and the
theme's composition broadened as the crisis broadened, from China-exposed
consumer names to the whole market. Figure~\ref{fig:covidsubgraph}
shows the two stages side by side.

\textbf{Choosing an operating point.} The four configurations of
Table~\ref{tab:precaudit} are best read not as competitors but as operating
points on a measured precision--recall frontier, and the audit
quantifies the choice between them. The lenient configuration is the maximum-recall
setting (8/10 at median $-1$ week; stream precision 37.9\%), appropriate
where a missed theme costs more than a noisy list, as on a screening desk
with a human downstream. The strict configuration dissolves the
ubiquitous-entity glue, cutting incoherent clusters from 89 to 54 and
lifting precision to 42.9\%, at the documented cost of roughly three weeks
of COVID lead. The negative-binomial configuration is the clearest
demonstration that two distinct notions of precision exist: its event-level specificity is the best of any
arm (chance $0.9/10$), yet its stream carries the highest template share
(44\%), because tightening the tail suppresses ordinary variance while
boilerplate, whose counts are large and systematic, passes the
tightened gate. The
template-filtered configuration dominates when both properties matter:
44.7\% precision, every real discovery retained, and the gold list
unchanged. A user choosing a configuration is choosing which error they can
afford; the audit turns that choice from judgement into measurement.

\textbf{Filtering as an operating point on the benchmark.} The audit's
labels can also be applied as a filter, and the frozen gold-list
machinery then quantifies the recall cost of precision directly. The
filters act on emerging lifelines only, so the fair baseline is the
emerging-only stream: 8/10 detections with median lead $-0.5$ weeks
(the canonical $-1$ week includes GameStop's earliest match, a
recurring-status lifeline; on the emerging-only stream its replacement
match arrives one week later). Table~\ref{tab:survival} reports the
operating points. Removing every TEMPLATE-labelled lifeline, 30\% of
the lenient stream and 22\% of the template-filtered stream, costs
zero gold-list detections in either configuration: real events
do not occur in the boilerplate class. Restricting the stream to
REAL-labelled lifelines costs exactly one detection per configuration,
and the losses are instructive rather than random: the UAW lifeline is
teacher-labelled INCOHERENT in both configurations, and the
template-filtered COVID lifeline likewise, so strict REAL-filtering
re-matches COVID to the vaccine-race lifeline three weeks late. The
placebo control tightens under every filter (expected chance detections
fall from 1.8 to 1.0--1.7 of 10), the signature of a genuine precision
filter: noise lifelines are removed faster than topical ones. The
distilled judge of Section~\ref{sec:precisionaudit} reproduces the
zero-cost result and slightly improves on it: under its labels the REAL-only
stream retains 8/10 in both configurations ($p < 10^{-5}$), because
the student departs from its teacher on the gold-conflicted
packs. Two of those three disagreements sit near its decision boundary
(REAL probability $\approx 0.49$, the mid-range where the calibration
check of Section~\ref{sec:precisionaudit} finds the judge
over-confident), so the survival of all eight
detections is a point estimate at one threshold; the retention curve of
Section~\ref{sec:precisionaudit} (Figure~\ref{fig:retention}) is the
robust reading.

cannot count as new detections, therefore withholds credit. A

relaxed after the fact. To summarise, FTX is missed for coverage,
ChatGPT largely for book-keeping, and the conservative scoring convention,
working as designed, is part of the reported recall.

\chapter{Experiment 3: Temporal Link Prediction under Point-in-Time Discipline}
\label{ch:exp3}
% Marking areas 2 & 4: ML benchmark + critical analysis; contextualises Exp 2.
\begin{microabstract}
This chapter presents Experiment 3. It defines the forecasting task and
protocol, describes the temporal model and the baselines, reports the
benchmark and its replication across the three graph constructions,
presents the velocity-feature ablation, the error anatomy, and the
retraining on the template-filtered substrate, and closes with results
and discussion.
\end{microabstract}

\section{Introduction}
Where Chapter~\ref{ch:exp2} asks what is emerging, this chapter asks
how far the graph's future can be predicted at all at the level of individual
edges, contextualising the theme system. The benchmark follows
the recurrence-baseline critique of \citet{Gastinger2024Recurrence}: the
training-free baseline is treated as a first-class competitor, uncertainty is
quantified, and the same benchmark is repeated on all three graph
constructions as a replication test.

\section{Task and Protocol}
The task is temporal knowledge-graph forecasting (extrapolation): for each
test quadruple $(s, r, o, t)$, rank all candidate entities for $(s, r, ?, t)$
and $(?, r, o, t)$ using only history strictly before week $t$. Performance
is Mean Reciprocal Rank and Hits@$k$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MRR} = \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{q \in Q} \frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}_q},
\qquad
\mathrm{Hits@}k = \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{q \in Q}
\mathbb{1}[\mathrm{rank}_q \leq k],
\end{equation}
averaged over both directions, under the conservative raw ranking of
Section~\ref{sec:kgembed} and with mean-rank tie handling (ties share the expected rank, so
count-based baselines are neither inflated nor penalised). Every result is
decomposed by a key property of the data: a test edge is recurring if
its exact triple appeared before its prediction week and novel
otherwise. On the canonical core 61.6\% of test edges are novel (the recurring/novel
label is identical for the two directions of an edge).

\section{Models}
The temporal model is an RE-GCN-style network \citep{Li2021REGCN}: a
relational graph convolution over each weekly snapshot folded into a
recurrent state, with entity representations re-evolved from their base
embeddings over a trailing window at each prediction time and a decoder
scoring candidate edges. One relational-convolution layer, of the R-GCN
family \citep{Schlichtkrull2018RGCN}, and the weekly GRU evolution
\citep{Cho2014GRU} are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_i^{(l+1)} = \sigma\!\left( \sum_{r} \sum_{j \in N_i^r}
\frac{1}{c_{i,r}} W_r^{(l)} \mathbf{h}_j^{(l)} + W_0^{(l)}
\mathbf{h}_i^{(l)} \right), \qquad
H_t = \mathrm{GRU}\bigl(H_{t-1},\, \mathrm{RGCN}(G_t, H_{t-1})\bigr),
\label{eq:regcn}
\end{equation}
where $N_i^r$ are entity $i$'s neighbours under relation $r$, $c_{i,r}$ a
degree normaliser and $H_t$ the entity-state matrix after folding week
$t$'s snapshot $G_t$ into the recurrent state; candidates are scored
against the evolved states by a DistMult decoder with a learned
temperature. Hyperparameters are selected by validation-based
early stopping on each core's own validation weeks (epoch 22 on the canonical
core), never transplanted across constructions.

\section{Baselines}
Five baseline families bracket the model. The training-free
recurrence baseline scores a candidate by how often the exact triple
occurred in prior history (\citealp{Gastinger2024Recurrence};
Equation~\ref{eq:recurrence}); popularity
ranks by global historical frequency. The static embedding ComplEx
\citep{Trouillon2016ComplEx} is trained on all pre-test weeks and scores
structural plausibility independent of recurrence; its bilinear predecessor
DistMult \citep{Yang2015DistMult} appears as the decoder form inside the
temporal network and the semantic scorer rather than as a separate baseline;
both scoring functions are displayed in Equation~\ref{eq:scoring}. The classical heuristics Common Neighbours and Adamic--Adar
\citep{AdamicAdar2003, LibenNowell2007} operate on a trailing twelve-week
subgraph, with the query entity excluded from its own candidate set
(Equation~\ref{eq:heuristics}). Finally,
ChronoBERT entity-name embeddings \citep{He2025Chrono} with a lightweight
trained scorer provide a point-in-time-clean semantic baseline.
Backoff hybrids use the recurrence rank for recurring candidates and a second
scorer for novel ones.

\section{Evaluation Protocol and Uncertainty}
All scorers observe strictly pre-$t$ history, with within-week
update-after-scoring ordering so no same-week information leaks. Confidence
intervals are 95\% paired bootstrap \citep{Efron1979Bootstrap} (1{,}000
resamples over test directions),
and, as a methodological lesson of this dissertation, small gaps are
additionally checked against training-seed replication, because query
resampling alone conditions on a single training run and understates
uncertainty (Section~\ref{ch:pit}). Concretely, with per-direction
reciprocal ranks $\mathrm{rr}_d$ (seed-averaged where seed replicates
exist) and resample index sets $I_b$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{MRR}}_b = \frac{1}{|I_b|} \sum_{d \in I_b}
\mathrm{rr}_d, \qquad b = 1, \dots, 1{,}000,
\label{eq:bootstrap}
\end{equation}
and the percentile interval of the $\widehat{\mathrm{MRR}}_b$ is reported;
a gap between two systems reuses the same $I_b$ for both, pairing the
comparison at the query level. The full benchmark is run on the 200k
core, the 600k core and the canonical deduplicated core.

\section{Ablation: Velocity Features in the Forecasting Model}
\label{sec:velablation}
The theme system of Chapter~\ref{ch:exp2} rests on link-formation velocity;
a natural question is whether the same signal improves link-level
forecasting. We test the hypothesis directly. Each entity-week receives
three trailing features from the detector's own machinery
(Section~\ref{sec:causalaccel}): $\log(1+O)$, $\log(1+E)$ and the clipped
surprise $z$, computed over the same four-week recent and 26-week baseline
windows. The features are linearly projected into the pre-activation of the
temporal network's weekly evolution step: the snapshot pre-activation
becomes
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a}_i = W_n\, \mathrm{agg}_i + W_s \mathbf{h}_i + W_f
\mathbf{f}_{i,t}, \qquad
\mathbf{f}_{i,t} = \bigl( \log(1+O),\, \log(1+E),\, \mathrm{clip}(z)/10
\bigr),
\label{eq:velinject}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{agg}_i$ the relational message aggregate and $W_f$ the only
added parameters. The baseline arm, with the
projection disabled, is bit-identical to the frozen benchmark model, and its
three-seed mean (0.120 all, 0.031 novel) reproduces the benchmark entry in
Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}. Both arms are trained under identical
hyperparameters and early stopping, three seeds each, and compared by paired
per-query bootstrap on seed-averaged reciprocal ranks (1{,}000 resamples
over the 49{,}620 test directions).

The result is unambiguous: velocity features degrade forecasting on

with the bimodal picture above: burst statistics announce that an
entity is becoming active, not which partner it will link to, and
injecting this attention signal into entity evolution perturbs the stable
co-occurrence geometry on which recurrence-dominated prediction relies. The
degradation is largest where that geometry does the work, on
recurring edges. One integration design is tested here (input-side linear
injection of three features); richer designs could behave differently, and
the claim is scoped accordingly. The direction is consonant with
evidence from general temporal networks, where \citet{He2024Stacking}
find that computationally expensive temporal topological features are
often no more accurate than well-chosen static features; the result here
sharpens that lesson for a relation-typed financial graph, where the
injected activity statistics are not merely unhelpful but harmful. Read together with Chapter~\ref{ch:exp2},
the ablation sharpens the division of labour: velocity is a detection
signal, not a forecasting feature.

\section{Retraining on the Template-Filtered Substrate}
\label{sec:tfretrain}
The audit of Chapter~\ref{ch:exp2} established that filtering template
articles improves the detection stream at no recall cost. This section
closes the loop in the forecasting direction: if the extraction layer
is cleaned, does edge-level forecasting improve? We retrain the
identical RE-GCN configuration (same protocol, hyperparameters and
three seeds) on the template-filtered core, whose substrate removes
25.1\% of articles and 16.9\% of triple instances.

The aggregate numbers move in the direction opposite to naive
expectation, and the decomposition below makes the reason precise. Within the filtered graph the test set is far less answerable by
memory: the recurrence baseline scores 0.157 where the canonical test
set gives 0.196, and the backoff system lands at a three-seed mean of
0.177 (seed spread below 0.002), while the network's novel-edge MRR is
essentially unchanged (three-seed mean 0.030 against 0.031). These
aggregates are not directly comparable, per the commensurability rule
used throughout (the two graphs differ in test set and in candidate-set
size, 19{,}374 against 21{,}216 entities); we perform a
paired comparison on shared queries. The filtered test set is in fact a
strict subset of the canonical one (21{,}133 of 24{,}810 queries), the
per-query reconstruction is validated exactly (novelty flags match
100\% and every published MRR is reproduced from the dumped ranks), and
the novelty labels agree on 99.4\% of shared queries.

are reported as measured, signed results rather than assumptions.

\section{Results and Discussion}
The picture is bimodal: the past is legible; the future is genuinely
surprising. Recurring structure is predicted at almost no modelling
cost, novel formations
resist every model family tested, and the best available lever on novel edges
is semantic knowledge of what an entity is rather than temporal or
structural pattern. Cross-construction comparisons of absolute MRR are
avoided throughout, since raw ranks over candidate sets of 21k--46k entities
are not commensurable; the defensible cross-corpus claims are the novelty
trajectory (72.4\% $\rightarrow$ 64.0\% $\rightarrow$ 61.6\%) and the
invariant method ranking. The failure of triadic-closure heuristics
(novel MRR $\approx 0.008$) is itself informative: the assumptions that hold
in social networks transfer poorly to a financial co-mention graph. These
results frame the theme system of Chapter~\ref{ch:exp2}: individual novel
edges are near-unforecastable, yet their aggregate acceleration is exactly
where exploitable signal lives. The ablation of
Section~\ref{sec:velablation} establishes the converse as well: feeding
that acceleration back into the link-level model only degrades it. The
substrate-level counterpart (Section~\ref{sec:tfretrain}) completes the
accounting: cleaning template noise out of the graph leaves per-edge
skill intact while deflating the aggregate benchmark, so part of the
apparent predictability of the unfiltered graph was boilerplate all
along. A final
slice analysis connects the two layers descriptively: partitioning the
frozen per-query test ranks by the precision audit's labels
(Section~\ref{sec:precisionaudit}) shows that forecasting skill
concentrates around real themes. Test edges touching members of
audited-REAL lifelines rank far better (MRR 0.193) than edges touching
only noise-theme members (0.064) or neither (0.058), and the gap survives
on novel edges (0.056 against 0.025 and 0.012, nearly twice the global
novel ceiling). Both caveats are disclosed: the membership sets span the
full period and the labels are post hoc, so this is descriptive rather
than deployable, and much of the lift is an activity effect (the REAL
slice is 51\% novel against roughly 70\% elsewhere). Two readings remain.
The legible part of the future clusters around genuinely emerging themes,
sharpening the bimodal picture; and the slice independently corroborates
the audit's judge, since a network that never saw its labels finds
REAL-theme regions three times more predictable than noise-theme regions.

\section{Point-in-Time Integrity}
\label{ch:pit}
\subsection{Two Kinds of Leakage}
Two distinct threats are conflated by the umbrella term ``lookahead bias'',
and this dissertation treats them separately. Graph and feature
leakage is the use, at prediction time $t$, of any data dated after $t$; it
is a property of the pipeline and can be eliminated by construction.
Extractor hindsight is subtler: the language model that built the
graph was trained on text postdating the corpus, so its extractions could in
principle encode knowledge of outcomes. It cannot be eliminated without
retraining the extractor; it can only be measured and bounded.

\subsection{Prevented by Design}
The first threat is addressed by mechanism rather than by manual
vigilance: publication-date
timestamping of every edge (the single most important control); chronological
train/validation/test splits fixed once; structural masking, so any model at
time $t$ observes only \Gle; strictly trailing windows and rolling causal
normalisation in the detection system; conservative raw ranking, so no
evaluation-time knowledge of other true future facts enters a prediction;
within-week update-after-scoring ordering; and validation-only tuning. Two
disclosed exceptions to the trailing-statistics rule are the static template
blacklist and the train-estimated NB dispersion of Chapter~\ref{ch:exp2},
both conservative in direction.

\subsection{Measured, Not Eliminated}
ICKG is distilled from GPT-4 and is not point-in-time clean. Residual
hindsight is quantified with four independent checks, chosen so that each
rules out a different contamination channel; they are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:pitchecks} and examined in turn below.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{The four extractor-hindsight checks and what each rules out.}

\midrule

\subsubsection{Apples-to-Apples Retraining}
Architectural comparisons can conceal data advantages: the early-stopped
temporal network holds out validation weeks that the static embedding
trains on, so a gap between them could reflect the split rather than the
architecture. The check retrains the temporal network on data identical to
ComplEx's, removing the asymmetry. On the sparse 200k core the two then
match exactly (novel MRR 0.025 for both): no comparison in
Chapter~\ref{ch:exp3} rests on an uneven split, and the temporal network's
later advantage on denser cores cannot be a data artefact.

\subsubsection{Time-Shuffle Placebo}
If a temporal model's score is unchanged when the order of its history
snapshots is randomised, the model is, by definition, extracting no
temporal-ordering information. This is observed on the 200k core: MRR
$0.090 \rightarrow 0.091$ under shuffling. The check is decisive for
interpreting the qualified null of Chapter~\ref{ch:exp3}: the absence of a
temporal advantage at that density is intrinsic to the data, not a tuning
failure, and no amount of hindsight in the extractor could manufacture
ordering signal that the shuffle shows is absent. The same check motivated
the density investigation, since a null that is intrinsic at one density
may lift at another, which is what the 600k rebuild found.

\subsubsection{Blinded Re-Extraction}
The most direct probe of extractor hindsight: re-extract a 300-article
sample with entity names and dates masked, so the model cannot recognise
who or when, and compare the output. The relational structure is retained
(valid triples per article $16.8 \rightarrow 21.8$; relation-mix
total-variation distance $0.10$), confirming that extraction is grounded in
the text rather than in recalled knowledge of the entities. The
distributional statement is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TV}(p, q) = \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{r} | p_r - q_r | = 0.10,
\label{eq:tv}
\end{equation}
over the fifteen relation shares $p$ (unblinded) and $q$ (blinded): the
two distributions can disagree on at most 10\% of probability mass. One family is
the exception: the causal ``impact'' relations lose roughly a third of
their share ($27.5\% \rightarrow 21.1\%$), so causal attributions carry
measurable entity-dependence, and hindsight versus naming-specificity is
not separable by this test. The concern attaches to impact-relation analyses
specifically, and the error anatomy of Section~\ref{sec:erroranatomy}
sharpens the boundary from the other side: the forecastability of impact
relations is unimpaired (their novel edges sit at or above the global
ceiling), so the entity-dependence concern is about how impact edges are
extracted, not about how they behave once in the graph.

\subsubsection{The Cutoff-Controlled ChronoBERT Comparison}
The sharpest instrument, following the capacity-controlled design of
\citet{He2025Chrono}: score the same link-prediction task with two versions
of the same model differing only in training cutoff, 2022 (blind to the
test year) against 2024 (which has ingested it). Any lookahead in entity
knowledge should appear as the 2024 model's advantage. The apparent gap
($+0.0010$ MRR at 200k) initially tested significant under the query
bootstrap; training-seed replication then showed run-to-run noise of the
same size (three-seed ranges fully overlapping, the later model not even
consistently ahead), and on the canonical core the gap is not significant
even before that correction ($+0.0005$, 95\% CI $[-0.0009, +0.0019]$). The
conclusion is a bound, not a detection: extractor lookahead is below
$\sim$0.002 MRR, well below the $+0.011$ semantic effect it could be
confused with. Query resampling that conditions on a single training run
can certify noise as signal; this means that small model-comparison effects need
training-stochasticity replication before they are believed.

\section{Summary of Findings}
\textbf{Building the Time-Indexed Knowledge Graph.} A reproducible,
point-in-time-clean temporal knowledge graph can be built from public news
with an open, locally hosted extractor at practical cost, and its dominant
data-hygiene hazard, syndication duplication in per-ticker corpora, can
be measured and corrected.

\textbf{Edge-Formation Velocity, Burst Detection, and Walk-Forward Theme
Discovery.} Link-formation velocity, causally scored and clustered on the graph, supports
a walk-forward theme-discovery system that detects 8/10 major 2020--2023
themes at a median lead of $-1$ week, far above a placebo chance of
$\approx 2/10$ ($p \approx 10^{-4}$), robustly across detector families,
dispersion assumptions and window choices. Lead time reflects the information
structure of the event: anticipatable themes are flagged weeks early, pure
shocks at the news-flow floor. A blind, validated lifeline-level audit puts
the high-recall stream's precision at 37.9\%, localises its largest
correctable share to template-bound clusters, and shows that filtering
template articles from the substrate lifts precision to 44.7\% at no cost
to recall. The audit's labels generalise into instruments: a distilled
lightweight judge reproduces them on held-out storylines, and removing
the labelled template class from the stream costs no gold-list
detection.

\textbf{Temporal Link Prediction under Point-in-Time Discipline.}
Edge-level forecasting is bimodal:
recurring relationships are highly predictable, novel ones sit near a low
ceiling for every method, temporal structure helps only as data density
grows, and semantic priors are the best lever on novel edges. Injecting the
detector's velocity features into the temporal network significantly
degrades forecasting on every subset, confirming that velocity is a
detection signal rather than a forecasting feature. Retraining the
network on the template-filtered graph adds the complementary
composition result: per-edge skill on shared queries barely moves while
the aggregate benchmark falls, because template noise inflates apparent
forecastability.

\textbf{Point-in-Time Integrity.} Extractor lookahead bias is bounded below
measurement resolution by a cutoff-controlled comparison, and query
resampling alone is shown to be an unreliable certifier of small effects.

\section{Strengths and Weaknesses}
The work's principal strength is the validation discipline around its central
claim: a frozen gold list with event-anchored windows, a placebo null, three
burst detectors scored against their own chance levels, dispersion and window
sensitivity, and replication of the benchmark across three graph
constructions. Its corresponding weakness is breadth over depth in places:
the lifeline-level audit of Section~\ref{sec:precisionaudit} rests on a
single judging model under a single rubric, anchored by controls and
twenty-four hand-labelled packs across two subsets; the two candidate second
judges failed the control gate, so no independent model corroboration
exists (the distilled small judge, trained on the teacher's own labels,
inherits rather than corrects this dependence); and the
velocity-feature ablation of Section~\ref{sec:velablation} tests a single
integration design, so its negative verdict is scoped to that design rather
than to every conceivable use of burst statistics in a forecasting model.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
Residual extractor hindsight is bounded, not zero, and causal impact
relations carry measurable entity-dependence. Corpus coverage: a
US-equity news corpus under-reports crypto and private-company themes,
which is why FTX is missed outright; the ChatGPT signal is instead absorbed
by a pre-existing lifeline under the conservative credit rule
(Section~\ref{sec:exp2results}). Detection is bounded by what the corpus reports. Duplication: exact-hash deduplication is a lower
bound; near-duplicates survive. GPE sparsity: country relations are
thin in a US-centric corpus. Metric comparability: absolute raw MRRs
are not comparable across candidate-set sizes, so cross-construction claims
are restricted to rankings and novelty rates. Temporal resolution:
all analyses run on weekly buckets, so lead times are quantised to whole
weeks and carry an inherent $\pm 1$-week jitter at bucket boundaries; the
$+1$-week news-flow floor observed for shock events is therefore partly a
resolution artefact, and a daily-resolution system might flag such events
within days. Finer bucketing is a data-density trade-off rather than a free
improvement: daily formation counts on this core would be too sparse to
support stable surprise baselines. Regime dependence: the
gold list is concentrated in 2020--2023; earlier, calmer regimes contribute
fewer validation events. Multiple testing: the gold list and windows
were frozen before evaluation, but ten events remain a small sample.
Static inputs: the template blacklist and the NB dispersion are
full-period or train-period statistics, disclosed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:exp2}.

\section{Future Work}
Five extensions follow directly. The velocity-feature ablation tested one
input-side injection of burst statistics; gated, decoder-side or
per-relation integrations would establish whether any design escapes its
negative verdict. At the extraction layer, the audit motivates prompt- or
fine-tune-level noise suppression: the extractor was run with its
training-matched prompt as a fixed instrument, but a retrained adapter
could be asked to emit canonical entity names, to ignore screener
boilerplate at source, or to attach per-triple confidence, each targeting a
failure mode this dissertation measured downstream. Entity resolution, shown by the crude-merge
bound to move novelty by roughly four percentage points, is the next
data-quality lever. The detection layer would benefit from a rolling template
blacklist, extended fingerprint coverage for the template-filtered
configuration (company-name skeletons are under-caught), and hierarchical
burst detection. Finally, the FNSPID price series enable a downstream study of whether
theme leads translate into investable signal; that application is treated
throughout as a use of the method, not its aim.

Pro 6000 GPUs (Chapter~\ref{ch:exp1}); every analysis, including the temporal

a single 6\,GB consumer GPU; the precision-audit judge additionally requires

PyTorch (CUDA), networkx, NumPy, SciPy and pandas. Each graph construction is
a directory of FinDKG-format flat files (entity, relation and time indices;
chronologically split quadruple lists; weighted edge-week table); an
environment variable selects the construction so that every module runs
unchanged on any of them (the three benchmark constructions and the
template-filtered detector substrate). The derived knowledge-graph dataset (triples
and indices) and the source code are released for replication. Article text
is not redistributed onward: FNSPID is distributed under CC BY-NC 4.0 and the
underlying articles remain publisher-copyrighted, so the release instead
includes the exact FNSPID row identifiers of every sampled article together
with the seeded sampling scripts, from which both corpora reconstruct
exactly from the public FNSPID distribution. Fixed constants: weekly bucketing over 365 weeks; splits
0--254/255--309/310--364; detector gate $z \geq 3$, $O_t \geq 6$; windows
$r=4$, $b=26$ (defaults); Louvain seed 42; lifeline matching Jaccard
$\geq 0.3$ with an eight-week gap; NB dispersion $\alpha = 3.75$; bootstrap
$B = 1{,}000$; placebo draws $100{,}000$. Audit constants: packs of the top
twelve members with up to ten source articles from the window
$[\text{birth}-4\text{wk}, \text{birth}+1\text{wk}]$, the top three carrying
450-character body ledes; judge Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-AWQ under greedy
decoding with a fixed prompt and a rubric frozen before judging; article
fingerprints (v2) with skeleton recurrence $\geq 25$ and entity
template-share $\geq 60\%$; Louvain resolution 1.0 (1.5 and 2.0 appear only

dropout 0.05, learning rate $10^{-4}$, three epochs, completion-only
loss, sequence length 3{,}072, seed 0; storyline groups by
cross-configuration member overlap (Jaccard $\geq 0.3$); held-out
